Real-Life Solutions for Waste Management

PBL
Practical action, community participation, and measurable results.
1 · Problem

Waste is growing faster than our habits

  • Increasing waste generation is putting pressure on existing waste-management systems.
  • Mixed waste makes recycling and recovery difficult.
  • Food waste causes odour, attracts pests, and adds methane emissions.
  • Plastic waste blocks drains and pollutes land and water.
  • Causes: poor segregation, convenience, limited awareness, weak collection, and no accountability.

2 · Empathy

Understand the people behind the waste

  • Residents: want clean streets; need bins, simple instructions, reliable collection.
  • Sanitation workers: need safer handling, protective equipment, less mixed waste.
  • Shopkeepers: need affordable alternatives to single-use packaging.
  • Schools: need easy routines students can repeat daily.
  • Waste pickers: need dignity, recognition, stable income, safer conditions.
Tools: interviews, observation, journey maps, “a day in the life,” waste audits.
3 · Define

How might we make correct disposal the easiest choice?

The solution should:
  • Reduce mixed waste at its source
  • Make separation simple
  • Work with existing infrastructure
  • Require limited investment
  • Encourage community participation
Users: households, students, vendors, cleaners, collectors, local authorities.
↓ Mixed waste↑ Clean recyclables↑ Compost↑ Participation↓ Litter
4 · Idea generation

Build a system, not a single bin

Three-stream bins
Wet/organic, dry/recyclable, and reject/sanitary waste.
Compost & recovery
Composting, repair and reuse, refill stations, donation and exchange, deposit-return points.
Participation
Waste champions, QR-based reporting, weekly challenges, local partnerships.

6 · Rapid estimation & concept

A 30-day pilot

1
Week 1: measure the current waste stream; map collection points.
2
Week 2: install labelled bins; train users; create a simple poster system.
3
Week 3: start composting and recycling handoff; check contamination daily.
4
Week 4: compare results; collect feedback; improve the process.
Example pilot: one school floor, 100 households, or one market lane. Track: kg of wet, dry and reject waste; participation; contamination; complaints.
7 · Rough prototype

Make the solution visible and testable

  • Build & label: a three-bin station from reused cartons or buckets, with colour-coded picture labels.
  • Compost & track: a ventilated container with dry leaves and food scraps; log waste diverted and contamination.
  • Route & test: prototype a collection route and handoff log; ask five users to sort ten items and explain their choices.
8 · Demonstration

Show the before-and-after journey

  • Before: mixed waste → difficult recovery → pollution and higher handling effort.
  • After: correct separation → composting and recycling → useful materials recovered → less waste to disposal.
  • Show a mixed bag, sort it into three streams, then display kilograms diverted, contamination, participation and complaints.
Dashboard measures are example fields, not reported results.
Conclusion

Waste becomes a resource when everyone owns the system

Prevent first
Refuse unnecessary items, reduce, reuse.
Separate next
Keep organic, recyclable and reject waste apart.
Recover value
Compost organic waste; recycle clean materials.
Measure & improve
Test with small pilots; improve with feedback.
Take action
One location, one team, three measurable targets for 30 days.
“Effective waste management is not the responsibility of one person or one department. It is a shared system where prevention, segregation, recovery, measurement, and community participation work together.”
